A menu-bar app for macOS, plus a Chrome extension.

Most antivirus checks a file after it's on your machine.

Aegis checks things before they reach you — and nothing risky ever runs on your computer.

Links, judged without opening them

Look-alike domains, hidden destinations, bad reputations and young sites — caught from the URL, its records and public threat feeds. The link is never opened.

Downloads, reverse engineered

Risky files are opened in remote sandboxes by two independent analysts, compared, and explained in plain English — before the file ever lands in your Downloads.

Scam text, spotted

Gift-card demands, fake support numbers and impersonated banks are flagged on the page — even when there's no link to click.

How a link gets checked

In about a second, as you browse — without ever loading the page.

The extension reads the page

Every link and plain-text address, plus new ones as the page changes.

The app checks each one

Look-alike and trick-URL rules, then Google Safe Browsing, the URLhaus and OpenPhish feeds, domain age, DNS and certificate logs.

You get a verdict on the page

Risky links get a red border; clicking one opens a guard that shows where the link really goes, so you decide before anything happens.

In the three places threats arrive

In Chrome

An extension flags dangerous links and scam text, and holds every download until it's been scanned.

On your Mac

A menu-bar app runs the checks, keeps the history, and watches your Downloads folder for files from other apps.

On your phone

Send a screenshot of a sketchy text or email by iMessage and get a verdict back.

Four honest verdicts

Aegis never says "safe." When it can't be sure, it says so.

MaliciousKnown-bad, or several strong warning signs. Blocked.
SuspiciousEnough warning signs to stop and check. Held.
UnknownA hidden destination or too little evidence. You double-check.
CleanNothing found. Opens normally.
